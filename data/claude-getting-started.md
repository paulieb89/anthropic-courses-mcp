 comfortably. That is the sweet spot for a first try.

 3. Where to start: your first 15 minutes
 A low-friction setup

                 Pick one real task
        1        Choose one thing you already need to do today. Example: draft a reply, summarize notes, or understand a
                 spreadsheet tab.

                 Give a little context
        2        Tell Claude who you are, what the task is for, and what good output looks like. Even 2 to 3 sentences helps a
                 lot.

                 Ask for a specific output
        3        Say what you want: an email draft, a short summary, a table of pros and cons, a step-by-step plan, or a
                 cleaner version.

                 Refine once or twice
        4        If the first answer is close but not right, ask for changes: shorter, clearer, more direct, more detailed, or
                 different tone.

                 Review before using
        5        Check important facts, numbers, names, and decisions. Treat Claude as a strong first draft partner, not final
                 authority.

  Prompt recipe: context + task + rules. Example rules: keep it under 150 words, use bullets, sound friendly but
  professional, cite sources, or ask me clarifying questions first.
